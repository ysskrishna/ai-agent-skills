# Six Thinking Hats: Facilitation Guide

## Standard 90-Minute Session Agenda

Times are **minutes from session start** (total 90 minutes).

| Minutes | Activity |
|---------|----------|
| 0–5 | Welcome & introductions |
| 5–15 | Context setting (review relevant background, data, framing) |
| 15–75 | Six Hats session (see hat allocations below — 60 minutes) |
| 75–85 | Action planning |
| 85–90 | Wrap-up and close |

### Hat Time Allocation (60 min total)

| Hat | Duration | Notes |
|-----|----------|-------|
| 🤍 White | 10 min | Start here to establish shared facts |
| ❤️ Red | 7 min | Brief — emotional check-in only |
| 🖤 Black | 12 min | Give this time; people have concerns |
| 💛 Yellow | 10 min | Often under-served — protect this time |
| 💚 Green | 12 min | Needs room to breathe |
| 🔵 Blue | 9 min | Synthesis + next steps |

Adjust based on group size. For 8+ people, add 2–3 minutes per hat.
For groups of 3–4, this can compress to 45 minutes total.

## Facilitator Script Prompts

### Opening (Blue Hat)
*"Today we're using the Six Thinking Hats method to explore [topic]. The key rule is:
one hat at a time — when we're wearing a particular hat, we think only through that lens.
Everything else gets parked until the right hat comes up. I'll keep us moving."*

### White Hat Prompt
*"White Hat on. Let's start with the facts. What do we know about this situation — actual
data, research, evidence? And what do we need to find out?"*

*Redirect if needed: "That's a great point about the risk — let's hold that for the Black
Hat. For now, just the facts."*

### Red Hat Prompt
*"Red Hat on. No need to justify anything here — just share your gut reaction. How does
this make you feel? What's your immediate emotional response?"*

*Note to facilitator: Don't push back on feelings. Don't let others argue with feelings.
Acknowledge and move on. Silence is OK — sit with it for a moment before prompting.*

### Black Hat Prompt
*"Black Hat on. Time to think critically. What are the risks, weaknesses, and obstacles?
What could go wrong? Let's be honest about the challenges."*

*After each point: "Good — and what would need to be true to mitigate that risk?"*

### Yellow Hat Prompt
*"Yellow Hat on. Now let's focus purely on the positives. What's the upside? Who benefits?
What's the best case? Why might this actually work?"*

*If the group is reluctant: "I know we've just been in Black Hat mode. I want us to
genuinely spend some time on what's good about this. What are the opportunities here?"*

### Green Hat Prompt
*"Green Hat on. This is pure creative thinking. No filtering, no criticism — we'll
evaluate in a minute. What alternatives haven't we considered? What if we completely
changed our approach? What's the most unexpected idea?"*

*Techniques to spark ideas:*
- *"What would a completely different industry do?"*
- *"What if we removed the constraint of [cost/time/approval]?"*
- *"What's the opposite of our current approach?"*
- *"If we were starting from scratch, what would we do?"*

### Blue Hat Closing Prompt
*"Blue Hat on. Let me reflect back what each hat gave us..."*

Then synthesise:
- White: key facts and gaps
- Red: emotional temperature of the group
- Black: top risks to address
- Yellow: strongest opportunities
- Green: most promising new ideas

*"Given all of that, what are the key decisions we need to make, and what are the
immediate next steps?"*

## Facilitation Tips

### Managing Dominant Voices
If one person is dominating, try:
- Round-robin: "Let's hear from everyone in turn for this hat."
- Nominal technique: "Write your thoughts first, then share."
- Redirect: "[Name], I want to make sure we hear from others. [Different name], what's your take?"

### Managing Silence
Silence often means people are thinking. Wait 10 seconds before prompting.
If silence continues: "Let's try this — what's one word that captures how you feel about this?"
Or: "Does anyone want to build on what [Name] said?"

### Managing Off-Topic Contributions
*"Great point — that feels like Black Hat thinking. We'll get there in [X] minutes.
Can we hold that thought?"*

Keep a "parking lot" for ideas that arise in the wrong hat section.

### Short-Form (30-Minute) Version
If you're short on time, run White → Black → Green → Blue.
- White (5 min): ground in facts
- Black (8 min): surface the real concerns
- Green (8 min): generate options to address those concerns
- Blue (5 min): decide next steps
- Buffer / discussion (4 min)

### Async / Remote Version
Tools like Miro, MURAL, or Notion work well for async Six Hats.
Structure: create a board with one section per hat. Give participants 24–48 hours to
contribute sticky notes to each section. Then run a synchronous Blue Hat session to
synthesise outputs together.

## Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Black Hat dominates the whole session | Visibly move to Yellow Hat: "We've been thorough with risks. Now let's spend 10 minutes ONLY on what could go well." |
| Red Hat gets skipped as "too soft" | Reframe: "Knowing how people feel predicts adoption. Let's take 5 minutes." |
| Green Hat produces only obvious ideas | Use provocations: "What would a 10-year-old suggest?" or "What's the most illegal version of this?" (then work backwards) |
| Blue Hat facilitator shares their own views | Remind: "While I'm facilitating, I'm saving my White/Black/Yellow views. When we do the hats, I'll contribute through them." |
| Group never reaches the Blue Hat synthesis | Reserve it — don't let discussion bleed into it. "We have 8 minutes left. Blue Hat now." |
